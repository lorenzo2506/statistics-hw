4. Discussion

    

      

        What we observe. The group generated by G is cyclic with order n (9 for the p = 17 curve,
        so 9G = O and the sequence repeats). The output depends deterministically on k, but the map
        k ↦ kG is very structured: (k+1)G = kG + G, so neighbouring keys give points related by
        one fixed addition, and the map is periodic with period n. Small k over a tiny field give
        visibly non-uniform, sparse coordinates; over larger fields x and y look roughly uniform, with
        a Pearson correlation between k and x or y close to 0 (no linear correlation), yet this is not
        security: it only reflects that the point jumps around the field.
      

      

        Meaningful for a one-way function (ECDLP). Easy forward computation (double-and-add,
        ~log k steps), hardness of the inverse, determinism, and a point that is always valid (closure
        of the group law). Also apparent uniformity of outputs and absence of simple correlations are
        desirable, but they are only necessary, not sufficient: here the real security comes from the
        size of the group and the hardness of ECDLP, not from "mixing".
      

      

        Specific to cryptographic hash functions. The avalanche effect (flipping one input bit
        changes ≈50% of output bits), collision and second-preimage resistance on arbitrary-length
        inputs, and fixed-size output for any input. The measurement above shows kG has no avalanche:
        k and k⊕1 give outputs whose bit-flip rate is not systematically 0.5 and which are
        algebraically related. Hence scalar multiplication is a one-way function but not a hash function.
